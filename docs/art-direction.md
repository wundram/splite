# First garden: painted miniatures

Confirmed direction: a painted miniature fantasy garden. The milestone is one
scene someone wants to linger in, with a readable character and convincing
materials. Completing engine features is not the visual acceptance criterion.

Keep the locked black void, near-top-down camera, afternoon light and one
biome. Work on a small vignette around the bridge and hero tree before adding
more map content. Large silhouettes first; splat softness should describe
leaves, cloth and moss, while stone and wood retain clear structure.

## First asset set

1. Player idle: warm cream face/hair or hat above a muted rust coat, deep blue
   lower body. Visible head, hands, feet and facing at the locked camera. Start
   with a static pose; validate shape before spending on walk-cycle fits.
2. Hero tree: an asymmetrical canopy with three readable leaf masses, visible
   gaps and warm trunk. Avoid a uniform green sphere. Tree footprint can exceed
   one tile; tree canopy remains a free volume.
3. Ground: a moss/grass edge, a worn stone path, and a pond bank with reeds.
   Keep traversable surfaces as meshes; use splats for soft growth and grit.
4. Dressing: two shrub profiles, cream/lilac flowers, warm gray rocks and a
   short wooden bridge. Use restrained variation in rotation, hue and scale.

Palette: muted sage/olive foliage, warm gray stone, dark walnut wood, restrained
cream/lilac flowers and one rust-red player accent. Reserve strongest color and
contrast for the player and interaction targets. Author roughness and painted
color variation into source materials, but capture albedo without baked sun.

## Acceptance sequence

- Compare source-model renders at the gameplay camera before fitting. Reject
  source assets whose silhouettes already fail. No detail budget can fix that.
- Fit one static tree/prop; retain source, capture, full fit, reduced fit and
  Godot screenshot together. Check silhouette, leaf gaps, ground contact,
  color under afternoon/moon/lantern light and freedom from floaters.
- Evaluate the same fits through isotropic versus anisotropic rendering before
  expanding the catalog. The existing renderer discards trained anisotropy;
  that can erase improvements made by the trainer.
- Fit the player idle, then construct a walk clip using shared normalization,
  pivot and framing. Independent frames must not pulse in size or shimmer.
- Dress the bridge/tree vignette and review day/night screenshots and a short
  walking clip at native resolution. Measure frame time on the target Mac.

Proposed initial budgets are experiments: tree 600/1200/2400, player 400/800/1600,
small foliage 100/200/400. Compare images and frame times instead of treating the
lowest number as a requirement. Current import reduction needs replacement or
post-reduction refitting if it destroys coverage.
